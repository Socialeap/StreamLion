**1. Recommended product**

Build a small, separate `/admin` entry within StreamLion's existing React/Vite application, served by Cloudflare Pages and Functions, backed by the existing D1 operational database. Reuse Google authentication and Stripe payment records. Keep customer project content authoritative in Google Sheets/Drive. The admin portal needs operational account metadata; it does not need access to customers' files, notes, recordings, transcripts, or OAuth credentials.

The owner signs in as `shakoure@transcendencemedia.com`. V1 supports account/payment/usage reporting, complimentary credits for an eligible individual account, and correction of the unused portion of a specific manual grant. Pilot administration may be linked to its existing controls; changes to pilot capacity, access, or policy are a separate scope.

Use three views: **Overview**, **Users**, and **Credit activity**. A user detail drawer preserves the table's search, scroll position, and filters. Avoid introducing a separate dashboard service or a second authentication system.

**5. Define frequency of use before building charts**

Recommend **active days in the last 7 and 30 days**, plus last activity and completed-action counts. An active day means at least one qualifying foreground app-use event or successful authenticated product action. Background polling, token refreshes, dashboard views, and admin grants do not count as customer use. Count the account once per day across all devices and features.

Reuse server-confirmed AI, speech, coordination, and relevant Google-save operations. Add a small, bounded first-party foreground-use signal for otherwise unmetered field work. Offline events queue locally and reconcile only to the same verified account, with deduplication, bounded age, and separate occurrence/receipt timestamps. Clear or isolate pending events during account switching. Treat client-reported events as observational; they never authorize billing, credits, access, or refunds.

Daily account summaries keep dashboards cheap and survive routine operation-log cleanup. Start with the dimensions required for 7/30-day reports; retain a provisional 90 days of daily usage and cumulative non-content counters, subject to the owner's retention policy before release. Report “Tracking since …” and coverage limits. Offline work not yet synced is unobserved, so “last active” cannot mean that no other work occurred.

Store raw timestamps in UTC. Default daily reporting to America/New_York. The Worker computes the IANA-timezone calendar date with a tested `Intl.DateTimeFormat(...).formatToParts` helper and persists `reporting_zone` plus a `YYYY-MM-DD` reporting date on daily summaries. Use a unique/indexed key including mode, subject, zone, and reporting date; SQL groups by that stored date rather than interpreting IANA names. A bounded, checkpointed Worker backfill computes the same dates without sending raw event history to the browser. Local-date range filters use these keys, and event-detail filters use matching UTC start/end boundaries computed by the same timezone-aware helper.

Use the server's issuance time to derive the New York calendar month for grant allowances; never accept an allowance month from a browser or backdate a grant using an offline-event timestamp. Test spring/fall daylight-saving transitions, repeated clock hours, 23/25-hour days, and month boundaries; never assume a fixed UTC offset or 24-hour local day. Any future timezone option must rebuild/query compatible daily buckets. SQLite's built-in date modifiers do not provide an IANA-zone grouping API. [SQLite date/time functions](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_datefunc.html).

**6. Credit accounting and grant policy**

Existing wallets remain authoritative for balances. The reporting layer combines completed `streamlion_shared_spends_v1` charges for AI/coordination with completed speech charges. Do not add AI turn charges a second time when a matching shared-spend row already represents that consumption. Migration `0009` copied only then-reserved AI turns, so earlier completed commercial AI turns may exist only in `streamlion_credit_turns_v1`. Reconcile legacy completed charges by mode, subject, and request ID against documented migration/event evidence, counting each once. An unexpected unmatched modern AI turn is an integrity finding, not automatically extra usage. If the legacy boundary cannot be established, label historical totals incomplete. Legacy pilot-wallet activity is separately labeled and excluded from commercial totals unless explicitly selected.

Pending reservations are not completed consumption. Failed operations with restored customer credits are not retained usage, even if provider cost was incurred. Distinguish gross purchased credits, refund/dispute adjustments, credits granted, completed use, in-flight reservations, and currently available balances. Keep spend and refund adjustments on their actual event dates when available; show limitations for historical states that lack event history. Use ledger/event changes for forward reporting so later refunds do not silently rewrite earlier-period metrics.

Grant **complimentary credits** without altering Stripe orders or purchased balances. **Confirmed owner decisions, October 24, 2026: preserve existing access rules; retain unused admin goodwill after Core refunds.** Require valid existing metered-feature eligibility for new positive grants and spending. Current pilots do not gain paid AI/speech access merely by receiving credits. Preserve all provider enablement, per-feature budgets, request limits, refund/dispute holds, and other access checks. The owner subsequently approved implementation with the limits and speech funding below. Live grants, deployment and new credentials remain separately held.

**7. “Add free credits” workflow**

1. Open an account drawer and choose “Add free credits.” Show the target email and account identity prominently.
2. Enter a positive whole-credit amount and a required reason, such as service recovery, onboarding help, or promotional goodwill. Suggest 240 credits by default if that amount fits the remaining policy allowance; otherwise require an explicit valid amount, without automatically shrinking the gift. Show feature eligibility, current balances, proposed new admin-goodwill balance, the account's monthly headroom, global monthly allowance remaining, and refund/access consequences. Reject values above the configured per-grant/per-account/global limits before confirmation and repeat those checks in the database.
3. Review one explicit confirmation containing recipient, credits, and reason. Submit with a unique operation ID tied to that exact payload. Editing the payload requires a new operation ID.
4. The server first verifies current owner permission and mode, then looks up the operation ID and checks its complete payload. An authenticated retry of an already-successful grant returns the original receipt before evaluating mutable recipient eligibility, balances, monthly allowance, or new-grant settings. A changed payload with the same ID is rejected. Only a new operation proceeds to target eligibility, fresh mutation authorization, policy, and ceiling checks. A single grant-event insertion, guarded by database triggers, checks eligibility/limits and atomically applies the admin-goodwill increment, allowance accounting, and audit receipt. If two identical first attempts race, resolve the duplicate by reading the committed, payload-matching receipt; do not issue a replacement grant.
5. Return a durable receipt with grant ID, actor, target, mode, amount, reason, timestamp, and balances captured for that operation. Refresh current balances separately because the user may be spending concurrently. A retry or double click never grants twice, including a retry after a refund, repurchase, policy change, or exhausted monthly allowance. Grant-write shutdown must preserve an authorized read path for existing receipts.

Audit records cannot be edited or deleted through the portal. **Include a “Reverse unused credits” action in v1**, attached to an individual manual grant. Show original, consumed, reserved, previously reversed, and currently reversible amounts; require a correction reason, explicit confirmation, and fresh mutation authorization. An immutable reversal event references the original grant and can deduct only its remaining unspent, unreserved credits. If simultaneous spending reduces that amount, reject the requested reversal and refresh; never create debt or touch purchased/starter funds. An account's loss of paid eligibility does not prevent the owner from correcting its unused retained goodwill. Fully spent grants cannot be reversed through this action. Their historical event remains visible; any other remediation is separately reviewed. Use the same atomicity/idempotency guarantees as positive grants. Monthly issuance counts original positive grants; reversals do not automatically refill the monthly allowance.

V1 has no free-form negative grants, general balance editor, bulk grants, or automated customer email. A future notification requires a separate owner instruction and existing email controls.

Use D1's supported transaction model explicitly: no `BEGIN`/JavaScript reads/`COMMIT` sequence and no database transaction held open across `await` or provider calls. A grant insertion's `BEFORE` checks validate the owner/authorization record, mode, eligibility, and per-grant/per-account/global integer limits; its `AFTER` work updates source balances, both account/month and global mode/month allowance rows, and audit/receipt state. A unique operation ID and unique allowance keys protect identity and budget accounting. Failed checks raise a database error and roll back all writes. Use a fixed `db.batch()` only where multiple prepared statements are necessary, with guards inside the database and explicit success-row checks. Do not substitute a wallet version check for the global monthly ceiling. No-op conditional writes are not automatic errors. [D1 batch transactions](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/worker-api/d1-database/#batch).

Durable Objects are not required by the proposed bounded grant operation and are not added to v1. Before finalizing implementation, prove the grant/reversal transaction on test D1 with simultaneous requests, allowance boundaries, duplicate operations, policy changes, and injected audit failures. If that fails, resolve the concrete database design defect before enabling mutations; do not introduce another service speculatively. StreamLion already uses triggers, conditional writes, and payment revision checks, so this extends an existing pattern.

**8. Owner-only authorization and privacy**

Enroll the owner through an explicit, reviewed bootstrap using the existing verified Google sign-in for `shakoure@transcendencemedia.com`, then bind permission to that account's immutable Google subject. A browser-submitted email, domain membership, URL parameter, local-storage flag, or “first person to visit” cannot enroll an administrator. Email remains a display/verification attribute. Google recommends `sub` because an account's email can change. [Google OpenID Connect](https://developers.google.com/identity/openid-connect/openid-connect).

Keep a small, mode-scoped owner permission record with revocation support. Check it on every admin API request and object access, using an authenticated server session. A missing session, missing schema/configuration, unavailable permission lookup, or revoked role denies access. Keep owner access independent of owning a customer Core license. Do not automatically copy the full pilot-admin list into the new permission set. This follows the default-deny, every-request checks in [OWASP authorization guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html).

Use secure HttpOnly cookies, strict same-origin mutation checks and CSRF protection, bounded validated JSON bodies, prepared SQL, allowlisted sorting, and per-owner request limits. Add a server-timed admin window with a 15-minute idle limit and a 60-minute absolute limit as initial defaults; background requests never extend it. This is separate from the current 90-day ordinary Google session. Account switching, expiry, or revocation clears the admin UI and blocks new mutations. [OWASP CSRF guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Cross-Site_Request_Forgery_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html).

**Actual mutation step-up is a release prerequisite.** A newly completed OAuth redirect, refreshed token, consent screen, or `select_account` is not proof that the owner just entered a password or completed MFA. Google's documented prompt values are `none`, `consent`, and `select_account`; do not rely on Gemini's suggested `prompt=login`. Google's current OIDC documentation supports requesting `auth_time` and `amr` claims when enabled in settings. During the authorized authentication preflight, determine whether validated ID tokens provide the required recent-authentication evidence; verify signature, issuer, audience, expiry, nonce, and exact owner subject before using such claims. Missing/stale claims fail closed for mutations; do not infer freshness from token issue time. [Google OIDC parameters](https://developers.google.com/identity/openid-connect/openid-connect#authenticationuriparameters).

If Google cannot provide enforceable freshness for this deployment, the proposed alternative is an owner-approved WebAuthn/passkey factor with `userVerification: required`, while Google remains the sign-in identity. No passkey is created or enrolled without explicit owner authorization. Bind a short-lived, single-use server challenge to the exact owner, mode, recipient, grant/reversal amount, and operation ID; verify origin/RP ID and user-verification evidence server-side and consume the authorization with the mutation. A receipt lookup is read-only and does not require repeating a completed step-up. Keep the portal read-only until an approved, verified method is working. [W3C WebAuthn](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/).

**Recovery runbook:** document an audited operator procedure using existing Cloudflare/GitHub control. First establish that operator access is independently recoverable from the affected Google account; if it relies on that same locked account, it is not an effective fallback. Diagnose an OAuth configuration fault separately from a compromised identity. Before any role change, require explicit owner authorization, an authenticated binding to the replacement Google subject, expected database/mode/old-owner checks, and an exact reviewed script at the approved SHA. Rebind atomically, invalidate prior admin authorization and compromised factors as appropriate, and record an audit receipt. No public bypass route, email-only enrollment, extra owner, or fallback credential is created automatically. If independent access does not already exist, keep recovery marked unproven until a separately authorized recovery arrangement is verified. Test the procedure with fixtures before release.

Return only allowlisted reporting fields; never return whole session rows, OAuth tokens, saved-card secrets, raw customer files, or unrestricted database queries. Admin responses use `no-store` and bypass public edge caches. Exclude `/admin` and private admin API data from the PWA offline fallback, service-worker caches, and local persistent storage; clear in-memory data on logout, identity change and idle/absolute expiry. Retain only an opaque operation UUID and mode in tab-scoped session storage until an uncertain mutation is reconciled. This marker contains no identity, financial data or credential, never authorizes a change, and blocks new changes until resolved. Production account data must not be loaded into public previews or fixtures. Update the account/activity disclosure and retention documentation before release.

**9. Small backend surface and efficient queries**

Proposed reporting endpoints: `GET /api/admin/session`, `GET /api/admin/overview`, `GET /api/admin/users`, `GET /api/admin/users/:id`, and `GET /api/admin/users/:id/credits`. Mutations use `POST /api/admin/users/:id/credit-grants` and `POST /api/admin/credit-grants/:grantId/reversals`, with dedicated challenge/verification endpoints for the approved step-up method. Authenticated account IDs are validated and resolved on the server; opaque IDs are not authorization. Foreground activity collection uses a separate non-admin endpoint with no power to change balances or access.

Add only the missing operational structures: account registry, owner permissions/admin windows, daily activity summaries, source-aware manual-grant balances and spend allocations, immutable grant/reversal/audit records, grant policy/allowance state, approved mutation-authorization records, and the minimal payment/credit event projection needed for reliable history. Reuse existing ledger tables rather than replacing them. Final table/migration names and sequence are assigned against current main during implementation, not guessed now.

Keep a small mode-scoped account activity row containing a non-null `last_active_at` (zero means no observed activity), updated only by qualifying user actions. Index `(mode, last_active_at DESC, google_subject ASC)` for the default view. Read a 25/50-account page from that index first, using a cursor containing both time and subject for deterministic tie handling; fetch/join reporting summaries only for that page. Do not sort a full ledger join. Activity may move between pages; expose the report cutoff and restart pagination on refresh rather than promising a transactionally frozen directory.

Use additional indexes on subject, mode/status/time, local-date buckets, grant target/time, and actual supported filters/sorts. Exact email and normalized prefix search are the initial search paths; arbitrary substring search requires separate query-plan evidence. Filtered totals and chart aggregates are separate bounded queries/rollups, not full-history work hidden behind a paged list. Verify `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN`, rows read/written, and latency for default, search, combined filters, and later pages at 1,000/10,000 synthetic accounts. Do not assume every filter combination can use the default activity index. Keep credit/detail read-after-write consistency when returning a mutation receipt. [D1 index guidance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/best-practices/use-indexes/).

Default to refresh on entry, filter change, or explicit Refresh. Optional foreground refresh can be one minute, pausing when the tab is hidden. Display an as-of time and use consistent report cutoffs. No per-keystroke full scan or dashboard-triggered Google, Stripe, AI, speech, or email request. Revenue is read from reconciled payment records; external reconciliation is a separately controlled operation.

Initial engineering targets, to be measured rather than promised: a first useful page within two seconds on a normal desktop connection, paged API responses near/below 500 ms at the representative workload, and one atomic grant operation followed by a bounded refresh. Record actual D1 rows read/written and latency before setting a live usage budget; do not buy a service or upgrade the Cloudflare plan automatically.
