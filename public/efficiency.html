The hidden work around the capture

Operating the camera is only part of a spatial capture project. Interpreting a client’s request, checking access, resolving questions on site and explaining the delivery all take time. When that information lives across emails, notes and messages, the provider has to reconnect it at every stage.

StreamLion brings the agreed brief, field tasks, client conversation and Completion Brief into one project workflow. The opportunity is straightforward: less time reconstructing context, and a clearer record of what was requested and completed.

1. Agree the brief before the visit

A vague request creates work before anyone reaches the site. Turn the brief into itemized tasks, review them against the source and clarify access with the client. A shared scope gives both parties a reference when new requests arise. The model allocates 12 minutes instead of 45.

2. Keep the scope beside the fieldwork

A locked room or unclear instruction can interrupt the visit. With project details, tasks and messaging together, the provider can record an exception and ask for clarification in context. The client can follow recorded progress. The model allocates 85 minutes instead of 120; client response times and physical capture conditions still matter.

3. Hand over the record you have already built

Review a provider-branded Completion Brief that connects the requested work, evidence, logged exceptions and deliverable references. The provider still checks its accuracy and shares the relevant delivery information. The model allocates 15 minutes instead of 60.

4. Reduce avoidable follow-up

Documenting inaccessible areas and agreed changes can reduce misunderstandings and some return visits. It cannot eliminate rework or guarantee that a dispute will not occur. The comparison assigns a rework allowance of 45 minutes to the traditional process and 5 to StreamLion. This is a planning assumption, not an observed return-visit rate.

Method & limitations

An estimate to test against your work

These figures come from the supplied StreamLion time-efficiency chart and article draft. They are an illustrative scenario, not a controlled study or measured customer benchmark. No sample size, timing protocol or field-study evidence was supplied.

The arithmetic is (45 + 120 + 60 + 45) − (12 + 85 + 15 + 5) = 153 minutes, or 2 hours 33 minutes. Dividing 153 by the original 270 minutes gives a 56.7% reduction in modeled time, rounded to 57% on the landing page.

Of that saving, 113 minutes come from briefing, execution and handover; 40 come from the assumed rework allowance. Without that allowance, the modeled difference is 225 versus 112 minutes. Rework is contingent and does not happen on every project.

Results vary with project size, travel, site access, capture equipment, client responsiveness and familiarity with the workflow. Travel, scan processing and third-party platform time are not separately quantified. Do not treat the totals as a complete schedule for every job. This full-workflow example is separate from the landing page’s editable admin-task ROI calculator.

Turn recovered time into capacity

At an illustrative opportunity rate of $100 per hour, 153 minutes represents $255 of time capacity. That is not guaranteed revenue, cash savings or profit. The practical benefit may be a calmer handover, space for another task or fewer hours spent on administration.

To assess your own result, time a comparable project across the same phases, record return visits separately and compare the totals. Use your own hourly value when estimating the benefit.
